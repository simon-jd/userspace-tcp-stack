# Evidence for measured congestion-control gaps

Each comparison uses the five registered paired transfers for its condition. The bottleneck queue is a token bucket filter (TBF). Its cumulative drop counter is sampled, so interpolating at the measurement boundaries can produce fractional counts. The ingress capture records packets before the bottleneck, and the receiver samples establish delivered bytes. [Primary windows by transfer](gap-transfers.csv) · [Aligned one-second observations](gap-windows.csv) · [Per-run flags and measurements](runs.csv) · [Packet replay](capture-replay.csv)

### BBR, 10 Mbit/s, 20 ms, 0% configured loss, 0.5 BDP

The stack retransmitted 2.384% of data segments at the median, compared with 2.069% on Linux. All five pairs showed more drops at the 12,500-byte bottleneck queue on the stack transfer. During the primary window the stack recorded 491–509 TBF drops, compared with 423.8–439 on Linux. Before the bottleneck, the ingress captures recorded 491–509 stack retransmissions and 425.3–439 Linux retransmissions. Both sides reached 12,112 bytes of backlog at the 99th percentile in every run. Netem recorded zero drops across all ten transfers. The additional queue drops account for the stack's extra retransmissions while both sides delivered about 9.42 Mbit/s. [Median-run overlay](plots/10-20-0-0.5-bbr.png) · [Queue and capture windows](gap-windows.csv)

The stack trace recorded 2,494 retransmissions in the five measurement windows, including 2,483 in BBR's PROBE_BW mode and 11 in PROBE_RTT. Neither endpoint population recorded a sender timeout in these runs. The 99th-percentile sampled pacing rate was 12.31 Mbit/s in each stack run and 12.15–12.22 Mbit/s on Linux. Both had a median congestion window of 44 segments. The captures and queue counters locate the extra drops. The 10 ms samples cannot establish which individual pacing decision filled the queue. [Mode-tagged retransmissions and pacing](gap-transfers.csv) · [Sender controls](network-records.csv)

Matching each ingress data packet to the complete downstream capture locates the drops more precisely. In repeat order 0–4, the stack lost 494, 493, 505, 509 and 496 first transmissions inside the exact ingress window. Linux lost 423, 429, 432, 426 and 439. All 4,646 missing packets carried 1,448 bytes. Every one was the second of two consecutive forward data packets captured at ingress within 100 µs of each other. All primary-window retransmissions reached the downstream capture. With a 250 µs cutoff, the same loss classification held. A tighter 10 µs cutoff accounted for 2,398 stack and 2,102 Linux losses. These packet counts use exact ingress timestamps and a lifetime downstream match, so they can differ from sampled queue deltas and the frozen 10 ms retransmission bins at a window edge. [Counts by transfer](bbr-shallow-pairs.csv) · [Checksums and cutoff sensitivity](bbr-shallow-packet-replay.json)

The denominator counts a full-capture group of exactly two only when its second ingress timestamp falls inside the primary window. Across the five pairs, 2,497 of 45,815 second packets went missing on the stack transfers (5.45%), compared with 2,149 of 45,017 on Linux (4.77%). The stack had more two-packet groups and a larger fraction of second packets missing in every pair. Lifetime missing data counts equaled the kernel TBF drop deltas in all ten runs. Matching used forward sequence, payload length, IP ID and TCP header fields, with each flow identified by its observed SYN. On each of the 20 captures, the physical packet count equaled both tcpdump’s captured count and its received-by-filter count, with zero kernel drops. Each fingerprint had an unambiguous outcome. Its downstream occurrences could be matched to earlier ingress occurrences on the shared host clock within the capture timestamp precision. The router capture timestamps identify where the loss appeared along the path. They do not measure the TBF token balance or the sender decision that placed two packets close together. ### BBR, 10 Mbit/s, 20 ms, 0.1% configured loss, 0.5 BDP

Median retransmissions were 2.510% on the stack and 2.280% on Linux. All five pairs had more drops in the stack transfer's bottleneck queue. The primary-window TBF drop counts were 500, 500, 498, 490 and 509 on the stack, compared with 453, 466, 442, 435 and 462 on Linux. Across the five whole transfers, TBF recorded 3,330 stack drops and 2,977 Linux drops, while the upstream netem recorded 131 and 132. The queue accounted for the larger difference between senders. The two interface captures span both qdiscs, so a particular missing data packet cannot be assigned to one of them from those captures alone. [Median-run overlay](plots/10-20-0.1-0.5-bbr.png) · [Primary-window queue and trace measurements](gap-transfers.csv)

In the exact ingress windows, 2,598 stack first transmissions and three repeats were missing downstream. Linux lost 2,357 first transmissions and four repeats. With 100 µs defining adjacent forward data packets as above, both exposure and second-packet loss fraction were higher on the stack in each pair. Pooled across the five runs, 2,542 of 45,232 stack second packets were missing (5.62%), compared with 2,297 of 44,654 (5.14%) on Linux. Unlike the zero-loss configuration, some packets outside this second-packet group also went missing. The stack trace recorded 2,594 retransmissions in PROBE_BW and seven in PROBE_RTT. Goodput remained near 9.42 Mbit/s on both sides. [Per-transfer counts](bbr-shallow-loss-pairs.csv) · [Packet matching and source digests](bbr-shallow-loss-packet-replay.json)

Full-capture matching accounts for the non-data traffic as well. In addition to 3,458 missing lifetime data attempts on stack transfers, three forward ICMP echo requests were missing. Linux had 3,108 missing data attempts and one missing echo request. All forward TCP control packets arrived. In each of the ten runs the combined deficits equaled the two qdiscs’ cumulative drops. In repeat order 0–4 the stack's extra missing primary-window data packets totaled 46, 34, 58, 55 and 47. Even assigning every lifetime stack netem drop to a primary-window data attempt and no Linux data loss to netem leaves at least 21, 3, 30, 33 and 22 additional stack data attempts lost at the TBF in the respective pairs. This is a lower bound from the measured counters. [Per-pair loss accounting](bbr-shallow-loss-pairs.csv)
